About

I am a graduate researcher at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill, working at the intersection of developmental science and measurement. My work asks a single question in two directions: whether the instruments we use actually capture what they claim to, and what follows for the interventions we design on top of them.

I came to research along a nontraditional route — community college, then UNC-Chapel Hill, then graduate study, alongside a full-time career in analytics and raising a family. That path is why I am careful about measurement. I have watched numbers get used to decide things they were never built to decide.

Currently I work on learning science and validation with the CLICK Research Group in the UNC School of Education, on early mathematics research with the Math Potentials Lab, and as a Senior Quantitative Analyst at SOMPO International. I am applying to doctoral programs in developmental science for Fall 2027.

Research Program

One question, approached from three directions

1 · Does the instrument measure what it claims?

Cross-cultural measurement and psychometrics

Large international household surveys produce the early childhood development statistics that shape global policy. But the items in those instruments were largely built on a model of caregiving as dyadic verbal stimulation between one adult and one child. In many families — in Lagos, in Accra, and in Durham — care is distributed across grandparents, older siblings, aunts, and neighbors. I audit what those instruments capture, partially capture, and miss entirely, using measurement invariance and item-level methods.

2 · What follows for what we build?

Intervention design and instrument construction

If the underlying model of good caregiving is wrong, the intervention built on it delivers the wrong thing. The PANDA Study is where I work this out in practice: designing a caregiver-facing digital intervention end to end, constructing and fielding the survey instruments that measure it, and validating them rather than assuming they work.

3 · How does any of it reach anyone?

Research-to-practice and policy translation

Measurement and design matter only if the findings reach the people making decisions. This strand covers policy briefs prepared through the Society for Research in Child Development, an evidence memorandum delivered to a U.S. Senate office, and systematic work on how research recommendations actually reach classroom teachers.

Current and Recent Work

Projects

Learning Science and Validation — CLICK Research Group

UNC School of Education · PI: Dr. Jeffrey A. Greene · August 2026 – present

CLICK (Critique, Learn, Investigate, and Construct Knowledge) studies self-regulated learning, epistemic cognition, learning analytics, and digital literacy. I define and document the tagging constructs used to classify instructional content, identify the theoretical and empirical evidence that grounds them, develop the criteria against which AI-generated classifications are evaluated, audit the validity of the resulting tags and derived measures, and ensure interpretations remain supported by the available evidence.

Instrument Evaluation — International Household Surveys

UNICEF MICS (Nigeria, Ghana) and Young Lives (Ethiopia, Peru) · 2026 – present

A critical reanalysis of the Early Childhood Development indicators used in nationally fielded household surveys. Phase one codes caregiving constructs drawn from African developmental psychology against individual MICS ECD module items to identify what the instrument captures well, partially, or not at all. Phase two tests whether items function equivalently across respondent populations. Four studies preregistered on the Open Science Framework.

OSF Profile

The PANDA Study

Parenting, Adversity, Neurodevelopment, and Developmental Assessment · Circle Lab, UNC-Chapel Hill · October 2024 – May 2026

A neuroscience-informed digital parenting intervention for caregivers of children ages 3–5, designed, built, fielded, and analyzed independently. I authored the questionnaire end to end — item wording, response options, scale selection, screeners, and survey flow — wrote and revised the IRB protocols, built the PANDA Learning Hub and a separate user-testing platform in HTML, and collected primary data across four phases in Qualtrics. Validation in R used composite scale construction, Cronbach's alpha, McDonald's omega, multiple regression, and nonparametric tests with effect-size interpretation. Supported by a Summer Undergraduate Research Fellowship and the David Bray Peele Memorial Research Award.
